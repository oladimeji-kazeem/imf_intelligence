INTERPRETATION AND LIMITATIONS
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Higher-exposure counts measure peer-relative signal breadth.
2. HHI measures the concentration of higher signals across the five engines; it is not a measure of economic risk.
3. Engine co-occurrence indicates that two engines have at least one higher classification in the same country.
4. Jaccard co-occurrence measures overlap relative to the countries with exposure in either engine.
5. Spearman correlations use country-level percentile measurements across 20 countries.
6. Correlations are exploratory. Twenty countries provide a small sample for dependence analysis.
7. Correlation does not establish causal relationships or prove that two dimensions are redundant.
8. Dimensions may share source data, measurement periods and underlying economic drivers.
9. No composite risk score, default probability or country credit ranking is produced.
